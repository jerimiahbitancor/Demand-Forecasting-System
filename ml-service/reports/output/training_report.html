8. Chronological Split (Fit / Validation / Test)

Split via model_service.three_way_split(): the data is cut by date, never shuffled and never per product, so every product's test period starts on the same day and no date is on two sides of a cut. The last 20% of dates are the test period (scored once, never used to choose anything). The last 20% of the remaining dates are the validation period, used only by early stopping to pick the number of trees. The rest is the fit period. The final model is trained on fit + validation.

9. Training Settings and Early Stopping

XGBoost builds trees one after another; each new tree corrects part of the error the earlier trees left (the prediction is the starting value plus the learning rate times the sum of all tree outputs). Training error keeps falling as trees are added, but after a point the extra trees only memorise noise and the error on unseen dates stops improving. Early stopping watches the validation period and keeps the number of trees where its error was lowest. The final model is then trained with that many trees on the fit and validation periods together, and scored once on the test period.

The learning curve below starts at a high error for the first trees (the cut-off top of the chart) and flattens as trees are added. Settings and periods are as recorded in the model's own _meta.json.

12. Final Evaluation Metrics

Headline metric: WMAPE (weighted mean absolute percentage error), on the test period only. It is all items missed divided by all items sold: a WMAPE of 28.1% means the forecast missed about 28 items for every 100 sold. The dashboard shows this as accuracy = 100 − WMAPE = 71.9%. MAE (average items missed per product per day) and RMSE (like MAE but large misses count more) are reported next to it. MAPE is kept only in the per-product table for reference, because one day with very low sales can make it explode.
